---
name: Make it sound human
description: Use when a draft (an email, post, bio or article) reads like a machine wrote it and should sound like a person. Not for drafting in their own voice from scratch (use Write like me).
---
**When to use:** "This sounds like AI", "make it less robotic", "loosen this up", "make it read naturally", or a draft they pasted that's stiff.

**You'll need:** The draft. Their writing preferences (memory_search) if it goes out under their name.

**Steps:**
1. Ask nothing yet: read the draft and note who it's for, the language, and how formal it should be (an email to a client is not a caption).
2. Mark the tells: stock openers and closers ("I hope this finds you well", "In today's fast-paced world", "In conclusion"); filler transitions ("Moreover", "It is important to note"); hedges stacked on hedges; abstract nouns doing a verb's job ("the implementation of" for "using"); every sentence the same length; lists of three by habit; bullets where a person would write a sentence; dashes and bold sprinkled for effect; a summary that repeats the text.
3. If more than a few lines have these, rewrite from the meaning, not line by line. Otherwise fix only the marked parts.
4. Rewrite: lead with the point; concrete words over abstract ones; active verbs; short and long sentences mixed; contractions where the tone allows; one idea per paragraph; cut whatever adds nothing.
5. Keep every fact, number, name, date, link and quote exactly as it was. Keep idioms and punctuation right for the language (not English habits in Dutch or German).

**Check:** Read it aloud in your head: would a busy person write this? Nothing factual changed, nothing was added, and it's no longer than the original unless they asked.

**Return:** The rewrite, ready to copy. Then one line on the main changes ("cut the opener, merged the bullets into two sentences"). No score, no essay.

**Stop and ask if:** It's meant to pass as someone else's work where that matters (a graded assignment, a signed statement), the tone they want isn't clear from the text or who it's for, or it's legal or medical wording where a small change could change the meaning.
