---
name: SEO check
description: Use before publishing a post, page or listing, to make it easier to find and to catch claims that ads law forbids. Not for writing the piece from scratch.
---
**When to use:** "Check this for SEO", "better title for this post?", "which hashtags?", "is this wording allowed?", or a draft they're about to publish.

**You'll need:** The draft (or its link: web_fetch). web_search or x_search to see what people actually search for and what ranks; memory_search for their audience and brand words.

**Steps:**
1. Note where it will appear (their site, a shop listing, LinkedIn, Instagram, Xiaohongshu, Zhihu, WeChat…) and the market (country, language): both change the rules below.
2. Pick 3 to 5 keywords: what a reader would type to find this. Check them with a search: are those the words people use, and what already ranks? Add a few longer phrases ("standing desk for small rooms", not "desk").
3. Title: the main keyword near the start, under about 60 characters for search results, and the habit of the platform (a question on Zhihu or Quora; a number or emoji on Xiaohongshu or Instagram; plain and specific for search).
4. Body: the main keyword once in the first paragraph and a few times in total, naturally; headings that use variations; a short meta description (under about 155 characters) for web pages; alt text for images.
5. Tags or hashtags where the platform uses them: 5 to 10, mixing broad, niche and long phrases.
6. Scan for claims ads law commonly bans or limits, in any language: absolutes ("best", "No. 1", "only", "100%", "lowest price anywhere", 最, 第一, 唯一, 顶级, 最佳); promises of results ("guaranteed", "instant effect", 保证, 立竿见影); health claims for things that aren't medicine ("cures", "treats", 治疗, 治愈); "free" or "flash sale" that isn't real. Offer a safer word for each ("best" → "one of our most popular"; 第一 → 领先).

**Check:** Every suggestion keeps the meaning; keywords came from a search you did, not a guess, or are marked as a guess; nothing was keyword-stuffed.

**Return:** Keywords (marked checked or guessed), the old and new title with why, body changes as a short list, tags, then flagged claims with replacements. End with "not legal advice" if anything was flagged.

**Stop and ask if:** You don't know the platform or market, it's a regulated product (medicine, supplements, finance, alcohol) where a wrong claim costs money, or they ask you to publish it: you suggest, they post.
